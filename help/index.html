    Getting started

    MaciPadiPhone

    1. Install the beta

    Stagehand is in beta on Apple TestFlight. Ask to join, then open the invite on each device and install Stagehand from the TestFlight app.

    2. Choose where your shows live (Mac)

    The first time you open Stagehand on a Mac, it asks "Where should Stagehand keep your shows?"

    • Use This Mac makes a Stagehand Library folder in your Documents folder.
    • Choose Folder… puts the Library on a drive or another folder. Choose an existing Library to open it.

    Each show (a production) is saved in the Library as one .stagehand file. Stagehand keeps a copy every time you save, so you can go back.

    3. Open an example

    The examples are the fastest way to learn. Each one is a complete show that is saved in your Library. Look around, change things, then throw it away.

    ExampleWhat is in it
    Tour Date — one bandOne band on tour: patch, stage boxes, outputs, in-ears, wireless, backline and a schedule.
    Festival — bands onlyTwo stages of bands, up to 56 channels, with a full running order.
    DJ Night — DJs onlyOne club room. Six DJs roll between Rig A and Rig B, with a B2B, a vinyl set, a laptop set and a hybrid live set.
    Festival — bands and DJsBands and DJs on one stage, a DJ tent with A and B rigs, and a rig that travels between stages.
    • Mac: go to Productions and click Examples. The Help menu has them too.
    • iPad and iPhone: on the home screen, tap Open an Example.

    Getting around

    Mac
    • Everything is in the sidebar on the left. To change its order, use View › Customize Sidebar…
    • To see the welcome screen again, use Help › Welcome to Stagehand.
    • Press ⌘Z to undo a change.

    Find Anything (⌘K)

    Press ⌘K, or click Find in a production. Type a few words to jump to:

    • any page, production, stage, show day or act
    • a patch line (type ch 12 to find channel 12), an output, an IEM or a backline item
    • an action: Add Act, Add DJ, the PDF exports and the examples

    Every word you type must be in the result, so fewer words find more.

    Settings

    On the Mac, open Stagehand › Settings (⌘,).

    • General: Dark or Light look, and Spacing. Compact fits more on each screen. Printed sheets and Stage View stay the same.
    • Library: change or show your Library, and choose what Stagehand offers when you plug in a USB stick or a drive.
    • Productions: how things look for you, for example box colours on the patch. These do not change the files you share.
    • Labels & Printing: your label printer and label style.
    • Documents & AI: your house style for PDFs (logo, paper, colour), and how documents are read.
    • iPhone & iPad: pairing and sync. See iPad and iPhone.

    On iPad and iPhone, the settings (look, spacing, ruler size) are in the iOS Settings app, under Stagehand.

    Productions: tours and events

    MaciPadiPhone

    A production is one tour or one event. It holds each act's patch, stage boxes, outputs and plot, and the schedule for every show day.

    • Tour: one band, many nights. Each city can change the band's patch just for that night.
    • Event: a festival or a one-off show. It has stages, and many acts on each stage.

    Make a new one

    Go to Productions, click New Tour or New Event, type a name, and click Create. On iPad and iPhone, the same buttons are on the home screen.

    Bring in your spreadsheets

    Mac only

    Click Import Spreadsheets and choose one or more .xlsx files. Stagehand knows these layouts:

    • a single-artist patch sheet with tabs such as INFORMATION, INPUT PATCH, STAGE BOXES, FOH OUTPUTS and MON OUTPUTS
    • a schedule sheet with Inputs and Running Order tabs
    • a festival patch sheet with a Festival Input Patch tab

    Other layouts are refused with a message. Imports always come in as a tour.

    Revisions

    A new production says Rev 0 · Draft. When you send paperwork out, click Issue Revision… and add a short note about what changed. Every sheet then shows the revision, for example Rev 5 · K7Q. The three letters are different for each issue, so two copies of "Rev 5" can be told apart on paper.

    If you change something after you issue a revision, Stagehand shows "+ changes since this issue". Click the revision label to see the history.

    Delete a production

    On the Mac, point at its card on the Productions page and click the × in the top-right corner (or right-click and choose Move to Trash…). Stagehand asks first. It goes to the Finder's Trash with its riders and saved versions: to get it back, open the Trash and choose Put Back, until the Trash is emptied.

    Acts

    MaciPadiPhone

    An act is a band or a DJ. Each act has its own pages:

    DJ Setup (DJs only) · Patch · Stage Boxes · Stage Plot · Outputs · IEMs · RF · Backline · Info

    Add an act

    • Mac: click Add act in the sidebar. To put it straight on a stage, open the stage's Overview and click Add Act.
    • Mac, a DJ: click Add DJ in the sidebar, under Add act. It opens on the DJ Setup page.
    • iPad: tap Add Act in the left column.

    Rename, copy or remove

    Right-click an act in the sidebar for Rename…, Duplicate, On the Bill and Remove…. Removing an act also removes its patch, outputs, plot, IEMs, RF and backline, and clears its place in the running order.

    Tour nights

    On a tour, each city is listed in the sidebar. A city's pages start as the band's standard setup. To change it only for that night, right-click a patch line and choose Change Just for… or Drop for…. Reset … to the Band's undoes this.

    Patch (input list)

    MaciPadiPhone

    The patch is the act's input list. Each line is one channel.

    ColumnWhat it means
    ChThe channel number.
    BoxWhere it plugs in, for example R3 = Red box, input 3. Lines with no box are home runs (HR1, HR2…).
    SplitThe festival split input. Shown only when the act is on a stage.
    Name, Mic / DI, 48V, Stand, NotesWhat it is, what picks it up, and what it needs.
    StereoPaired with the next channel.
    OwnThe artist brings it.

    Useful buttons (Mac)

    • Add Input and Remove.
    • Renumber numbers the channels 1, 2, 3… in the order they are listed.
    • From the Boxes lays out the patch from the act's stage boxes.
    • Suggest… gives a starting input list. Check it with the band.
    • Place on Split puts the act on the festival split.

    Tick several lines to change their box, mic, stand, colour or 48V in one go. Shift-click ticks a run of lines. Drag lines to reorder them.

    Warnings. If a box input is used twice, a box does not exist, or a line runs past the end of a box, Stagehand shows "N lines to check". Point at it to see why.

    Stage boxes

    MaciPadiPhone

    Each stage box has a colour, a letter, a number of inputs and returns. Stagehand draws it to scale and shows which inputs are used.

    • Use + to add a box. Pick a Box type, or choose Custom to set the inputs, rows and returns.
    • If you change a box's colour, the patch follows. For example, R1 becomes Y1.
    • Save This Box as a Type… keeps your own box for next time.

    Where it lands

    Tell Stagehand how the box reaches the consoles: Split to two racks (FOH and MON each have a rack), Shared rack, or turn on FOH mixes monitors too for one console. Console show files use this.

    Labels

    The Label (Brother P-touch) card prints a label that lines up with the XLRs on the box. See Printing and labels.

    Outputs, IEMs, backline and counts

    MaciPadiPhone

    Outputs

    The FOH outputs and MON outputs are separate lists: wedges, IEMs, fills, subs, mains, records and more. Quick Add adds common sets, and From the Rig copies the stage's own outputs.

    IEMs

    One line per pack: name on the pack, mix, who wears it, system, band and frequency. Add from MON Outputs makes packs from your IEM mixes. Right-click a pack to add a spare. The Labels panel prints pack labels.

    Backline

    List what each act needs, who brings it (Artist, Hire, House or Shared) and its status. Hire List collects everything to hire. Request… makes an email draft for your supplier. You check it and send it yourself.

    Counts

    Mac

    The Counts page totals mics, DIs, stands, wireless and cables across the show, with spares. It shows what is short, and PDF… exports the list.

    Stage plot

    Mac: full editoriPad: rough layoutiPhone: view

    The plot is drawn on a 1 m grid, with the audience at the bottom. The stage's width and depth come from the stage's Overview.

    • Drag items from Add (musicians, mics, amps, wedges, DJ booth…) and Risers.
    • Drag to move. Drag the round handle to turn. Hold Option to turn by single degrees.
    • Add from Patch puts the act's mics and DIs on the plot.
    • Templates saves and reuses plots.
    • Trace a Plot… puts the band's own plot picture underneath, so you can copy it. Read the Plot places the icons for you. Check the highlighted ones.

    Shortcuts: ⌘D duplicate, ⌘⌫ delete, ⌘[ / ⌘] turn 15°.

    Festival stages and splits

    Mac

    In an event, each stage in the sidebar has pages for the whole stage:

    • Whole Patch, Whole Outputs, Whole IEMs, Whole Backline, Whole Plots, Whole Boxes: every act on the stage, side by side. At once on Whole Backline is how many must be on the stage at the same time, including the gear on all the DJ rigs.
    • DJ Rigs: every DJ rig on the stage, with who plays on it. See DJ sets.
    • House Patch and House Boxes: the festival's own lines, such as MC mics and ambient mics, and its own boxes.
    • Split Panels: the number of panels, channels per panel, and a 2-way or 3-way split. The panel view marks what needs re-plugging between acts.
    • Split Matrix: a read-only table of what is on each split input, for each act.

    To add a stage, go to Stages and click Add a Stage. Make a Running Order makes a show day for that stage.

    Schedule and show days

    Mac: editiPad and iPhone: view and run late

    Each show day has a running order. Set the Advertised start and the Curfew, add acts and changeovers, and Stagehand works out the times.

    The tabs

    • Running Order: the list of lines: doors, sets, changeovers, crew calls, curfew.
    • Timeline: the day as blocks. Drag a block to fix its time.
    • All Days: every show day in one table.
    • Calls by Person: put names in the Who column, and each person's calls show here, ready to text or email.

    How the times work

    • Sets fit backwards from the curfew, inside each act's minimum and maximum set length.
    • A grey time is automatic. Type a time to fix it. Clear it to go back to automatic.
    • A warning shows if a set is too short or too long, or the show runs past curfew.

    Add a day

    Use the Day menu: add a copy of this day, start from a template, save a template, or copy this day to other days.

    Running late

    On the Mac, use Push show late (+1, +5, +10) in the Stage View Operator. On iPad and iPhone, use the Schedule's Running Late menu. The act on now gets longer, and everything after it moves later. The curfew does not move, so watch for the past-curfew warning.

    Stage View

    Mac runs itiPad and iPhone show itAny browser

    Stage View puts a big clock, now and next, and time left on any screen side of stage. You run it from the Mac at front of house.

    Open it

    On the Schedule page, click Stage View. Two windows open: Stage View (the screen) and the Stage View Operator (your controls).

    Send a message

    1. Click a one-tap button, for example 5 minutes or Wrap it up, or type your own.
    2. Choose how it shows: Banner, Full Screen or Flashing, and Normal or Urgent.
    3. Choose which screens get it, then Send Now (⌘↩).

    Edit Buttons… changes the one-tap messages.

    Timer and lateness

    Set Timer runs your own countdown. Back to Schedule goes back to the running order. Push show late moves the show later.

    STOP THE SHOW goes to every screen and beats everything else, even while the operator is locked. Click END STOP to clear it.

    Screens

    • Main Screen is the Stage View window. Put it on a second monitor or a TV.
    • New Window adds another screen with its own look.
    • Each screen can have a Layout (Stage, Dressing Room, FOH, Monitor World), a text size, and the parts you want: clock, current act, next act, curfew, set list and more.
    • Free Layout lets you drag the parts where you want them. You can save layouts.

    On an iPad or iPhone

    Pair the device with the Mac first (see iPad and iPhone). Then tap Stage Screen on its home screen. The Mac must have Stage View open. To leave, double-tap the screen and tap Exit.

    In any browser

    In the Stage View window, click Share… › Share on This Wi-Fi. Scan the QR code, or open the address on any phone, tablet or laptop on the same Wi-Fi. For the band shows only doors, sets and curfew. The link stops after 12 hours, or when you close Stage View.

    Wireless (RF)

    MaciPadiPhone

    Each act has an RF page, and the Wireless page shows every channel on the site. The RF page has four tabs: Coordinate, Devices, Monitor and Scans and TV.

    Coordinate

    1. Choose the Country. Stagehand keeps frequencies inside that country's legal ranges. A show day can use a different country (Schedule › Day details).
    2. Add channels with Add Channel, or Add From the patch's wireless lines or the IEM mixes.
    3. Click Coordinate…. Choose Robust, Standard or More Frequencies, then Use These.
    4. Lock the channels you want to keep. Recalculate… redoes only the unlocked ones.

    These are quick checks. For a big or busy show, coordinate properly in SoundBase, Wireless Workbench or WSM. Export… sends your list to them, and Bring Back Frequencies… brings the result back (Mac).

    Scans

    Mac

    Add a scan from RF Explorer, Wireless Workbench or Sennheiser WSM (CSV, TXT or TSV). Or connect an RF Explorer or tinySA over USB for a live spectrum. Tick TV channels and add exclusions for ranges you must not use. An iPad can show a paired Mac's live scanner.

    Send to receivers

    Send to Devices… links each channel to a real receiver and sends the new frequencies to it. A mic or pack on the old frequency loses its link until you change it too.

    RF Network

    The RF Network page shows every receiver on your network, for any show. Sennheiser EW-DX, EW-D and Digital 6000 are found by themselves. Add Shure digital, UHF-R and Sennheiser G3/G4 with Add by IP. You can rename, retune, mute and change power, and Coordinate can check for clashes.

    Nothing shows? Allow Local Network for Stagehand in your device's settings, and be on the same network as the receivers.

    DJ sets

    MaciPadiPhone

    A DJ act has a DJ Setup page. Open the DJ Night example to see one.

    Set up a DJ

    1. Add the DJ (Mac: Add DJ in the sidebar).
    2. Choose a preset: 4 Players and Mixer, 2 Players and Mixer, 2 Turntables and Mixer, Laptop and Controller or Hybrid Live. The Booth Layout draws the gear from above.
    3. Change the DJ Gear if you need to. Each row is a list of common DJ gear (players, mixers, turntables, controllers and more), each drawn to its real size. Not on the list? Choose Type Your Own…. The gear also goes on the Backline page and the hire list.
    4. Choose the monitoring: Booth Monitors, IEMs, Booth and IEMs, or Headphones Only. The Outputs and IEMs pages update for you.

    Back-to-back (B2B): click Add DJ on the DJ Setup page to add a row for each DJ in the set.

    The booth

    • Table: pick a common size, shown in metres and feet: a 4 ft, 6 ft or 8 ft table, or a staging deck. Or choose Custom and type the size. There is no one standard: 6 ft is the usual pro booth, and the working height is about 0.85–1 m.
    • Monitors (each side): Wedge, Wedge on a Sub, Texas Headphones (a small line array on a sub), or None. The stage plot draws what you pick.

    A and B rigs

    Under Rigs On This Stage, click Add Rig to make Rig A, Rig B and so on. With two rigs, the next DJ sets up while the last one plays. With Plays On set to Auto Roll, each DJ takes the next rig in the running order. Stagehand tells you if a rig is missing gear the DJ needs.

    A rig can travel to other stages (Moves To). Stagehand warns you if it is needed in two places at once.

    How the rigs change over

    Under Rigs On This Stage, choose a Changeover for the stage:

    • Side by Side: both setups stay put, and the next DJ walks to the other one.
    • Two Rolling Tables: each setup is on its own rolling table. The next one is set in the wing, then rolls to centre as the last one rolls off.
    • One Table on a Roller: both setups sit on one long table, which slides so the next setup is centre.

    The stage plot, the changeover notes and the DJ Rigs page all follow this choice. With rolling tables or a roller, the live setup is always centre, with one pair of booth monitors fixed there.

    From the stage: tie lines and show control

    On the DJ page, From the Stage counts the lines the DJ needs run from the stage: Line (XLR), SDI (video) and Cat5 / Cat6 (network), with notes. Turn on Uses show control if the DJ syncs lighting or video, and say how, for example "Pro DJ Link to the lighting desk".

    DJ rigs on the stage plot

    A DJ's Stage Plot draws the rigs on the stage for you, as they are now: this DJ's own setup on the rig they play, the other rig's kit, the booth monitors, and how the table rolls. It redraws itself when a rig, the changeover or the DJ's gear changes; anything you drew by hand stays. Redraw DJ Rigs (Mac) does it on demand.

    On the stage's Whole Plots page, every DJ shows their setup, drawn with the real gear on each booth, even if nothing has been drawn yet. When the stage rolls between rigs, a big A or B in the corner says which rig the DJ plays. A line under each DJ's name says what changes for them: gear to swap in (for example a different mixer), what they bring, their tie lines and show control. If one setup is shared all night, it shows one table with its gear and no letter.

    See the whole night: DJ Rigs

    Each stage with DJs has a DJ Rigs page (Mac: under the stage in the sidebar. iPad: in the left column. iPhone: in the stage's list). It shows every rig side by side: its gear drawn from above, and the DJs who play on it, in time order, with set times. A travelling rig shows on each stage it visits. Missing gear and clashes show in orange. Tap a DJ to open their DJ Setup.

    Where DJs show up

    • The running order and changeover notes say which rig to roll to.
    • Stage View can show a Next DJ Rig tile on crew screens.
    • The stage plot gets a DJ booth.
    • Export DJ Sheet… makes a PDF of the setup (Mac).

    Reading riders and documents

    Mac

    In a production, click Read a Document… and choose a file: PDF (scanned is fine), Word, text, a picture, or a spreadsheet.

    1. Stagehand works out what it is (rider, stage plot, schedule or hire quote) and which act it is for. Change these if they are wrong.
    2. For a rider, click Read It. It reads with Stagehand's built-in reader on your Mac. If you turn it on in Settings, it can also use Claude with your own Anthropic account.
    3. Check what it found. You can edit the inputs and backline before they are used. For an existing act, choose to update only what changed, replace the patch, or add after it.
    4. Click Use These. The rider file is kept with the production.

    A stage plot picture goes under the act's plot, so you can trace it.

    Sharing, PDFs and printing

    PDFs and exports (Mac)

    In a production, open Share & Show Files. You can export everything as one PDF, or just the patch, stage plot, backline, schedule or day sheets. Export This Day's Packs… puts every band on the day in one PDF. You can also export a spreadsheet, add the schedule to your calendar, and make FOH and MON console show files.

    House Style… adds your logo, paper size and colour to every PDF.

    Advance packs (iPad and iPhone)

    Open an act and tap Share:

    • Advance pack: a PDF of every sheet, or only the sheets you choose.
    • Share as web page: one file the venue opens in any browser, with no login and no app. You choose which contacts are on it. Anyone who gets the file can see those contacts and can send it on. To update it, send a new file.

    Label printers

    DevicePrinters
    MacBrother P-touch over USB (found by itself), or a Brother P-touch, Brother QL or DYMO LabelWriter set up in macOS. Choose it in Settings › Labels & Printing.
    iPadBrother P-touch over Bluetooth. Pair it once in iPad Settings › Bluetooth.

    What you can print:

    • Stage box labels that line up with the XLRs (Stage Boxes page). Reprint Changed prints only the labels that changed.
    • IEM pack labels (IEMs page, Labels panel).
    • Console labels: a strip of fader names as one tape (Console Labels). On iPad it can fill from the patch.

    No label printer? Use Save as PDF… and print from Brother P-touch Editor.

    If you plug in a printer after Stagehand is open, quit and reopen Stagehand so it finds the printer.

    iPad and iPhone

    Build the show on the Mac. Then carry it on the iPad and iPhone at the gig. Sync uses your local Wi-Fi. There is no account, no cloud and no internet needed, and the sync is encrypted.

    Pair once

    1. On the Mac: Settings › iPhone & iPad, turn on Share on this Wi-Fi. A QR code shows.
    2. On the iPad or iPhone: tap Get from a Mac, choose your Mac, and scan the code.
    3. Choose a production to get.

    Both devices must be on the same Wi-Fi. If you click New Code on the Mac, every device must scan again.

    Send changes back

    Tap Sync with … in the production, or pull down on the home list to sync everything. These changes go back to the Mac: patch edits, schedule times and the stage plot. Other changes made on the phone stay on the phone.

    Attachments (rider files, plot pictures, PDFs) are not sent to the iPad or iPhone.

    USB sticks and console show files

    Mac

    USB Check

    Is a stick safe to plug into the desk? Choose the stick and the desk, then click Check Stick. The answer is Good, Check first or Don't plug in. Export Report… saves the result, and Make a Clean Copy… makes a clean copy of the stick.

    USB Check needs Full Disk Access. Turn it on in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Full Disk Access.

    Show Files

    Finds show files from Allen & Heath, grandMA, DiGiCo, Yamaha and Avid S6L on a stick or in a folder. Bring In files them by act, console and date in your Library, and every version is kept. Put on USB… writes one back.

    Convert Show (beta)

    Turns a show file from one console into another, for example SQ to dLive. Your original is never changed.

    A converted file can be wrong. Always open the result in the console's own software and check it before the show.

    Tour Backup

    Copies show recordings from a tour drive into your Library, and checks every copy.

    Common questions

    Where is my data?

    On your own devices. Stagehand has no account and no server. See the Privacy Policy.

    My iPad cannot find my Mac.

    • Check both are on the same Wi-Fi, and that Share on this Wi-Fi is on in the Mac's Settings.
    • Allow Local Network for Stagehand on the iPad (iPad Settings › Privacy & Security › Local Network).
    • Some venue and hotel Wi-Fi blocks devices from seeing each other. Try a phone hotspot or your own router.

    I changed something on my iPhone, but the Mac does not have it.

    Only patch edits, schedule times and the stage plot go back to the Mac. Make other changes on the Mac.

    The past-curfew warning showed after I pushed the show late.

    That is correct. Pushing late keeps the curfew where it is. Shorten a set or a changeover to fit.

    How do I undo?

    Press ⌘Z on the Mac. Stagehand also keeps a copy of the production every time it saves.
